## Reader's note (added by hand, not machine-generated)

The probe could not reach the question it was built to answer. `lookup_all_players.php` was
never called for any club, because no club survived the teams step — so **this report says
nothing about the quality of TheSportsDB's player data**. It says something more important.

**`lookup_all_teams.php?id=4659` returns English League 1.** Not a subset, not a partial
match, not stale NIFL data — a completely different competition, 24 English clubs, every row
self-describing as `idLeague: 4396 / "English League 1"`. The league id in the request is
being ignored or mis-served.

This is the exact hazard CLAUDE.md rule 3 was written about, observed live rather than
remembered:

> **Guard against wrong-league data.** TheSportsDB has served cached example data (English
> Premier League) instead of league 4659.

Two things follow.

1. **The existing guards are load-bearing and correct.** `api/table.js` and `api/events.js`
   both validate `idLeague === "4659"` before trusting a row and fall back to `data.js` on
   any doubt. Against this response they would reject everything and fall back — which is
   the right outcome, and means the live site is not showing English League 1 fixtures.
   Nothing needs fixing there; this is the guard doing its job.

2. **Squad lists cannot be built from this endpoint** in its current state. Whether the
   player data would be good enough is unanswerable and, for now, moot.

Worth checking before any future attempt: whether the same wrong-league substitution affects
`eventspastleague.php` / `eventsnextleague.php` (the endpoints the live site actually uses).
The guards would mask it silently — the app would just quietly serve `data.js` forever while
appearing healthy. A short follow-up probe of those two endpoints would establish whether
the live-data layer is genuinely working or has been failing over unnoticed.
